# Data collection workflow

WIMNA's primary job is to preserve what the simulator actually did. A stint may be driven by Assetto Corsa's player-car AI, a human, or both across a pit handoff. All observed samples remain simulator measurements. The controller is a recorded condition, not an evidence category or a reason to discard tire, fuel, energy, or thermal workload data. Synthetic replay fixtures remain labeled `SYNTHETIC` and cannot enter measured totals.

Two lap boundaries were recorded: a pit outlap and a timed lap. Each was native invalid with one cut; neither supplies a clean pace baseline or a tire comparison. There was no human stint, tire change or pit handoff. The test proves a useful continuous trace can be collected, while the matched multi-compound workflow still needs a live check. After recording ended and 0.4.0 switched AI off, the unattended car was seen stopped against a barrier in the UI. ## Collect tire stints

1. Choose the installed FH26 car and the exact track/layout. Use **WIMNA AI Thermal** when you want native AI control; use **WIMNA Test** or Practice for human driving. Hold setup, assists, multipliers and energy configuration consistent where possible, and retain a separate record for settings the app cannot read. The AI mode forces extended physics, so a direct comparison with a normal-mode human run needs an explicit compatibility check.
2. In the WIMNA overlay, enter a tire test label such as X/Y/Z, requested compound, condition label such as baseline or hot, and optional setup/profile label. Press **Start Collection** or **Start AI Data Collection**. These are driver-entered requests. The recorder saves the native observed compound, air and track temperatures, session mode and extended-physics readback separately. A matching setup/profile label is not a verified setup hash.
3. Prepare tires and fuel in the pits, then drive the stint. The recorder continues through warmup, flying laps, pit entry and handoff. Lap quality, native validity, pit state, controller readback, resets, pauses and recording gaps remain visible in raw data. Complete as many laps as needed to characterize the condition; collection has no automatic elapsed-time or accepted-lap cutoff.
4. To swap tires, bring the car into the pits under control and press **Mark pit / control handoff**. The marker and **End Stint / Box** only mark data; they do not switch control. If AI is driving, stop the car first and have a human ready before pressing **Stop AI Driver at Rest**. The app hides that action while native speed is above 2 km/h or unavailable, and waits for native AI-OFF readback after requesting it. Change tires with the supported pit workflow, update the labels and press **Start Next Stint**. Raw telemetry continues between stints. An observed compound change or native session reset closes the active stint and requires a new start.
5. Repeat for the other compounds and heat conditions. Export the collection from the companion. Keep at least one human competitive run under recorded conditions to anchor absolute driver pace; AI laps can provide repeated relative tire and workload observations. More matched repeats improve the comparison, but the app does not force an arbitrary lap count.

In the 0.4.1 candidate, **End Collection Run** closes recording and leaves AI control unchanged. If native AI readback remains ON, let the AI drive until the car is stopped, then use the separate **Stop AI Driver at Rest** action with a driver ready. You can instead exit the Assetto Corsa session through its game menu while AI remains in control. Neither the end button nor the pit marker brakes or parks the car. This safe-end path is source-tested and awaits an in-game check.

## What the analysis does

The export groups raw samples into physical stints and reports the exact car, track/layout, compound request and readback, controller share, environment range, fuel, energy and tire channels, lap times and quality flags. Native tire wear and KERS fields retain their native units and meanings until verified. Missing channels remain null. The custom thermal model uses recorded speed and driving workload with disclosed low/base/high assumptions; it does not claim to measure hidden electrical temperature or native power loss.

Pace use requires a nearly complete lap trace with stable control. A native-invalid AI lap with zero reported cuts can enter an exploratory tire contrast but is never labeled a clean comparable lap, because the native invalidity cause is unknown. Contact and spin detection are not independently verified by the current adapter; a native-valid lap with no recorded flags has that observability limit. Thermal projections marked `computed_partial` cover only recorded segments and do not reconstruct heat through gaps. `collection_samples.csv` is a convenience view of available-quality values; expanded raw JSONL retains each sample's unit, source and quality and is the modeling source, whether exported plain in 0.4.0 or as gzip JSONL in 0.4.1.

Tire comparisons use matched stints where possible. Track/layout, car and profile, fuel/energy state, temperature, tire age and controller are covariates rather than automatic filters on raw data. An unmatched difference remains descriptive and does not become a causal tire gain. Human clean laps supply a separate competitive pace anchor. Applying an AI relative tire effect to that anchor is a model assumption that needs a held-out human check before being presented as validated.

The old C/R/F/E/M/V campaign is still available as a structured test protocol, but collection stints do not have to follow its block order or lap targets. Case estimates and Plan C scenarios are screening inputs downstream of the data. No model coefficient is tuned to make Plan C win.
