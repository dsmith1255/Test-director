# AI thermal check

The WIMNA app has a separate **AI Thermal Check** run. It records Assetto Corsa's player car while the built-in AI drives. This is a diagnostic outside the Spa, Silverstone GP, Imola and Monza driving program. AI laps do not satisfy any C/R/F/E/M/V lap requirement.

## How control works

The installed CSP Lua-app SDK defines `physics.setCarAutopilot(active, chooseTyres)` for the player car and says that using it invalidates the lap. Select the separate **WIMNA AI Thermal** Content Manager mode for this diagnostic. It combines `ALLOW_PHYSICS_ALTERATIONS=1` with `FORCE_EXTENDED_PHYSICS=1`; the ordinary **WIMNA Test** campaign mode does not force extended physics. This matters on stock Spa, whose `surfaces.ini` does not activate extended physics. The app calls direct autopilot only when `physics.allowed()` confirms permission at runtime, then waits for `car.isAIControlled` to change. If permission is unavailable, the app tries the native `ACTIVATE_AI` command once and reports rejection or timeout. The configured **Toggle AI Driver** control under Content Manager Controls → Misc is an unverified manual fallback on this installation: C was bound, but the prior local attempt did not turn native AI control on. The app accepts AI samples only after an observed `isAIControlled=true`; request acknowledgement is insufficient.

CSP's optional `ALTERNATIVE_AI_DRIVER` setting makes the AI driver switchable off, and `AI_DRIVER_KEEP_SETTINGS` keeps it from altering car settings when that mode is active. Confirm both and check the observed fuel, tires, map and other preparation values before comparing traces. WIMNA does not edit CSP configuration, track `surfaces.ini`, RSS files or car physics to enable this check. The external Custom AI memory-mapped controller is unnecessary here and has separate track permissions.

## Run it

1. Start an offline **WIMNA AI Thermal** session with FH26 and the intended track. Start the portable companion and open the WIMNA in-game app. The overlay must show a granted autopilot permission and **Extended physics readback: true** for automatic control. Select **WIMNA Test** or Practice for the driver campaign. The AI diagnostic mode changes the session's physics setting, so do not compare its traces directly with campaign laps as if conditions matched.
2. Set the intended fuel, tires, weather and car settings. Press **Start AI Thermal Check**. The app requests AI control automatically. Keep your hands off the driving controls during the takeover check. Wait until the overlay shows **AI control readback: ON** and the car moves under native AI before treating samples as AI-driven. If the app reports a rejection, stop the diagnostic and resolve the capability before collecting AI data.
3. Let the AI complete a settling lap, then as many whole diagnostic laps as needed for the thermal workload trace. Watch for pit stops, resets, crashes, setting changes and recording gaps. There is no campaign clock or accepted-lap counter for this check.
4. End the diagnostic recording without switching AI control. If AI remains ON, keep the AI driving and exit the Assetto Corsa session through its game menu, or stop the car and have a driver ready before pressing **Stop AI Driver at Rest**. The app blocks its AI-OFF request while native speed is above 2 km/h or unavailable. The stop action does not brake or park the car; verify native OFF readback before driving manually. Export the run in the companion.

The export contains the raw AI-controlled speed, throttle, brake, tire and environment traces and a separate AI thermal analysis. It marks gaps, missing channels, observed control state and any native-invalid laps. Keep the trace separate from Tyler's driving pace and validation laps.

## What it can establish

AI driving supplies actual in-simulator load and cooling-air inputs for a repeatable thermal model exercise. The local CSP recorder may also expose tire, water or oil temperatures; the AC water value is documented as an approximation. Generic KERS channels must retain their native units until RSS semantics are verified.

The WIMNA electrical and cooling temperatures, warning/derating thresholds and Plan C airflow factors are custom engineering assumptions. A modeled temperature rise or requested restriction is **analysis-only** unless a native matching component sensor and a supported power control are independently verified. AI laps cannot calibrate an RSS electrical failure limit, prove Plan C reliability, measure Tyler's pace or show that the car actually lost power. The useful pass check is: AI takeover is observed, a contiguous trace is saved, measured ambient air is available, and the low/base/high thermal calculations replay deterministically with their provenance intact.

## API evidence

- The [CSP Lua SDK repository](https://github.com/ac-custom-shaders-patch/acc-lua-sdk) says the complete definitions for a given build are shipped in `extension/internal/lua-sdk`; the installed `ac_apps/README.md` defines `physics.setCarAutopilot()`, `physics.allowed()` and `ac.trySimKeyPressCommand()`, and `ac_apps/lib.lua` defines `car.isAIControlled`.
- [CSP's 0.2.5 changelog](https://acstuff.club/patch/?info=0.2.5) documents both `[RULES] ALLOW_PHYSICS_ALTERATIONS=1` for new-mode app Physics API access and `[TWEAKS] FORCE_EXTENDED_PHYSICS=1`. Its installed Frozen Drive new mode uses both switches.
- CSP's [Content Manager control defaults](https://github.com/ac-custom-shaders-patch/app-csp-defaults/blob/main/ext_controls/cfg/cm_controls.ini) list `ACTIVATE_AI` as **Toggle AI Driver**.
- CSP's [general settings](https://github.com/ac-custom-shaders-patch/acc-extension-config/blob/master/config/general.ini) document `ALTERNATIVE_AI_DRIVER` and `AI_DRIVER_KEEP_SETTINGS`.
- CSP's [Custom AI documentation](https://cup.acstuff.club/docs/csp/other-things/custom-ai) describes the separate external-controller route and its track opt-in requirement.

The 0.3.1 local Spa smoke test did **not** activate AI. Its recorded permission result was false, the native `ACTIVATE_AI` command was rejected, and `car.isAIControlled` stayed false. The user drove the car in that run, so its motion is not AI evidence. The 0.3.2 WIMNA Test launch likewise reported a denied permission result on stock Spa. In 0.3.3, the separate AI mode recorded zero AI-controlled samples both on stock Spa and with a temporary track opt-in; the original Spa file was restored afterward. The 0.3.3 app combined its API gates into one status, so that status alone does not identify which gate failed.

WIMNA 0.3.4 was installed through Content Manager and launched with FH26 at stock Spa/default. Its independent probe found `physics.allowed()` callable and returning `true`, `physics.setCarAutopilot` available, and extended physics active. The installed CSP exposes `physics.allowed` as callable `cdata`; the prior strict Lua `type(...) == 'function'` gate prevented the direct call. The 0.3.4 call turned native `car.isAIControlled` ON at simulation time 0.020 s and OFF at 22.039 s. The saved run `20261021152814-23330696` has 440 AI-controlled samples and 131 subsequent samples, about 20 Hz, no sequence gaps or reported drops, and a maximum observed speed of 312 km/h. No full lap was completed, and all samples remain diagnostic. The stop was requested while the car was moving; after control was released with no human steering, it coasted off track and was damaged. Assetto Corsa was exited after the run was saved. This verifies takeover and stop on the local installed CSP build, not on Tyler's separate installation or a native thermal power reduction.
